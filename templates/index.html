    How our AI works

    Every valuation goes through the same six steps.

    1. Car detailsWhat you enter in the form
    2. Data preprocessingClean text, work out vehicle age
    3. Feature encodingTurn brand, model and city into numbers
    4. Gradient boosting regressionPredicts the log of the price
    5. Estimated market valuePlus the range around it
    6. Valuation analysisVerdict, factors and projection

    R² score

    How well the model explains why prices differ between cars. {{ '%.3f'|format(meta.test_metrics.r2) }} means about {{ (meta.test_metrics.r2*100)|round|int }}% of the price variation in unseen cars is explained. 1.0 would be perfect.

    MAE

    The average size of a prediction error, in rupees: ₹{{ '{:,.0f}'.format(meta.test_metrics.mae) }}, or {{ '%.1f'|format(meta.test_metrics.mape_pct) }}% of the price on average. Some cars will be off by more, which is why every result shows a range.

    What it learned from

    {{ '{:,}'.format(meta.n_rows) }} listings from {{ meta.data_period }}. The model is a {{ meta.model_name|lower }} regressor, picked over ridge regression and random forest by 5-fold cross-validation.

    What it deliberately ignores

    Columns like the listing's own price rating and pre-discount price were removed, because they contain the answer. A model using them looks accurate in testing but fails on real users' cars.

    Model performance is based on the project's held-out test dataset.
